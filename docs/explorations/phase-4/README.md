# Phase 4 explorations — three one-page skeletons

Same content (verbatim from Phase 3), same visual system, three different skeletons. Built from `_build.py` (content lives once, in Python data; each variant only changes order, hero, product pattern, athlete pattern, nav and display moment) on top of the shared `_base.css` / `_base.js`. Screenshots in `docs/screenshots/phase-4/explorations/` (390 and 1280, `reduced_motion: reduce`, full page). Heights are `document.body.scrollHeight`; Phase 3 for reference: **10 802 px (12.8 viewports) at 390, 7 813 px (9.8 viewports) at 1280**. Targets: ≤ 5.5 and ≤ 5.

## Verdicts

**A — Directo.** The first product tile is on screen at 880 px on a 390 px phone (scroll one) and the whole product grid fits the second desktop viewport; the sheet only costs height when someone asks for it, so the page stays at 5.5 / 5.1 viewports with every section still readable, and contact is reached after four short sections. Drop-off risk is the lowest of the three because nothing is hidden behind a horizontal gesture and the section that sells (products) comes before the section that explains (Compromisso). What it costs: the athletes lose their large portraits in the closed state (72–96 px thumbnails, the 4:5 photo only appears on `+ n`), and an accordion that opens below a 2×2 grid needs a scroll nudge on mobile so the sheet is seen at all. Chosen.

**B — Rail.** The split hero and the giant `03` are the strongest first two screens on desktop, and the left rail frees the top of the page. But on a phone the rail collapses back to a top bar, the split hero costs a full extra screen (photo above, type below), and the scroller shows 1.5 of 4 products at a time: the two products most likely to be bought (kit, belts) are the only ones visible, the others depend on a horizontal swipe that many people never do. The always-open sheet adds ~1 000 px before the athletes. Result: 7.9 viewports on mobile, the tallest, and the horizontal scroller also leaks 74 px of overflow at 390 in this skeleton. Rejected for drop-off, kept as the reference for the `03` display moment (reused in A's Compromisso) and the sliding rail dot idea (reused as the sliding top-bar dot).

**C — Ficha.** A tabbed spec sheet means zero clicks to see a full product (gallery, sizes, order button) and the `KU01` at 160 px is a genuinely good display moment for the products screen. The cost is that the sheet is permanently on the page: products alone take 2 662 px at 390 (3.2 viewports) before the strip, Compromisso and athletes even start, so everything after products is pushed below 4 screens, and the name-tab athletes section is compact but dry (one photo at a time, the other two athletes are just words). 7.8 viewports on mobile. Rejected; its "always one open" behaviour survives in A as the `#p-kit` deep link (a shared link opens the sheet directly).

## Why A, and what changes when it becomes `index.html`

- Products first, closed accordion, roster athletes, contact in the footer band: shortest path from hero to contact, nothing behind gestures.
- Trims still needed to get under the targets: Compromisso panel (546 → ~430 px at 390) by dropping the aside stack on mobile, roster rows (1 044 → ~850 px) by putting the two readouts beside the name at 390, contact band (718 → ~560 px) with the heading at the 88 px scale instead of 96 and less top padding. Strip stays.
- Motion: everything in `_base.css`/`_base.js` (type-on labels, tick flash, sliding nav dot, scroll-driven hero, view()-timeline drift on the small photos, `ler mais` / `+ n` grid-rows reveals, gallery dialog fallback) carries over unchanged into the single file.
